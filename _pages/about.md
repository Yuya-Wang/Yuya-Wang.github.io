My research lies at the intersection of corporate finance, innovation, and industrial organization, with a particular interest in how firms make strategic investment and acquisition decisions in increasingly complex technological environments. I combine large-scale textual analysis, patent data, and financial markets data with modern machine learning methods to study how information, technology, and organizational capabilities shape firm behavior and value creation.

My current job market paper examines **technological complexity** as a fundamental characteristic of innovation. I develop a novel network-based measure of industry technological complexity using U.S. patent data and show that complexity has important implications for corporate policies and innovative outcomes. Firms operating in technologically complex industries adjust their financing and R&D strategies differently from firms in simpler technological environments, leading to distinct patterns of patent production, knowledge creation, and firm performance. This project provides new evidence on how the structure of technological knowledge influences corporate decision-making.

A second major research project studies the **economic motivations behind mergers and acquisitions (M&A)**. Rather than treating all acquisitions as homogeneous events, I construct a new transaction-level classification of M&A deals based on firms' merger announcements and press releases. Using large language models together with expert validation, I classify acquisitions into four primary motivations—**Efficiency, Finance, Growth, and Technology**. This new framework allows researchers to distinguish fundamentally different acquisition strategies and provides new insights into how deal motivations affect announcement returns, post-merger outcomes, and long-run firm performance. The project also establishes a scalable methodology for incorporating advances in artificial intelligence into empirical corporate finance research.

More broadly, my research seeks to better understand how technological change, organizational strategy, and capital markets interact to shape firm value and economic growth.
